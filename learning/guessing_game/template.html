The Guessing Game

Real text from Chotu's held-out pages, one token at a time. You get 10 chips to bet on what comes next. Three real models bet too. When the token is revealed, everyone gets a surprise score: low if you bet a lot on the right card, high if you didn't. Chotu's training loss is exactly this score, averaged.

Tap a card to add a chip (or press 1–6), then Reveal (Enter). Chips you don't place are spread thinly over all 49,152 tokens, so revealing with no chips is the same as blind guessing. A · means the token starts with a space. You pick from a shortlist of 6; the models pick from all 49,152.

The training run travels from left to right

The models' average surprise over 16,384 held-out tokens they never saw counted. The whole 15–25 hour H100 run is the journey from the grey dot to somewhere near the green one, and the kill rule's red line sits between them.

All bets and scores were computed for this page from Chotu's own data: the passages and the common-word counts come from the held-out shards (49M tokens counted from 13 shards; passages and the 16,384 scored tokens from the 14th). Untrained Chotu is the real 180,925,056-knob model with random starting values. SmolLM2-135M is a trained model of almost the same size that uses the same tokenizer; it read about 200 times more text than Chotu will, so treat it as a rough finish line, not a promise.
